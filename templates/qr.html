What is a QR code?

A QR (Quick Response) code is a two-dimensional barcode that can hold far more data than a traditional 1D barcode — URLs, plain text, Wi-Fi credentials, contact cards, and more. A phone camera or scanner reads the pattern of black-and-white squares and decodes it instantly, which is why they've become the standard for menus, payments, event check-ins, and product packaging.

Common uses

  • Sharing a link — printed materials, business cards, posters that need to point to a website without typing a URL.
  • Wi-Fi sharing — encoding network credentials so guests can join by scanning instead of typing a password.
  • Contact info / vCards — scan-to-save contact details at events or on business cards.
  • Restaurant menus, payments, check-ins — anywhere a quick scan replaces manual entry.

Why this one is high-definition

This generator uses High error correction (Level H), which means up to 30% of the code can be damaged or obscured (a logo overlay, a smudge, a fold) and it will still scan correctly. Combined with a large box size (20px per module), the output PNG is generated at high resolution — sharp enough to print at larger sizes without pixelating, unlike QR codes generated small and then stretched.

Tips for a scannable code

  • Keep a quiet zone (white border) around the code — this generator includes one by default.
  • Don't shrink it below roughly 2×2 cm for print, or the modules become too small for a camera to resolve at typical scanning distance.
  • High contrast matters — stick to black-on-white unless you know what you're doing with color contrast ratios.
